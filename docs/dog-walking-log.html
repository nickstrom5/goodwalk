How to keep a dog walking log (and a streak)

A dog walking log answers one question honestly: how much did the dog actually get walked this week? Here is what to record, three ways to keep it, and how to run a streak that survives real life.

What to write down

  • The date. One line per walk, not per day, so two short walks both count.
  • Minutes. The number that matters most. Published exercise guidance for dogs is given in minutes a day, so minutes are what you compare against.
  • Distance, if it is easy. Nice for totals ("47 miles walked with Rex"), not essential.
  • Who walked, if more than one person shares the job.

That is enough. Routes, weather and mood are optional extras that make a log harder to keep up.

Three ways to keep the log

Paper on the fridge

A calendar and a pen. Everyone in the house can see it and it costs nothing. The downsides: no totals unless you add them up, and it is not with you at the end of the walk.

A notes app or spreadsheet

Free, flexible and easy to total. It depends on you remembering to open it, which is the step that tends to slip.

A dog walk tracker app

An app can ask you at a set time, time the walk, and do the sums. There are free walk trackers on the App Store, some built around a map of your route. Good Walk is not that: it has no map and never asks for your location. It is built around a daily target, a reminder and a streak. Pick the kind that matches what you want to know.

Dog walking streak rules that work

A streak is only useful if it keeps you going after a bad day. These are the rules Good Walk uses, and they work just as well on paper:

  • Any walk counts. A day with at least one logged walk keeps the streak. Ten minutes in the rain still counts.
  • Count full-target days separately. That way the streak rewards showing up and the target still means something.
  • Today is not broken until it is over. No walk yet at 3pm is not a failure.
  • A missed day is just a missed day. The streak resets. The miles, hours and history stay.
